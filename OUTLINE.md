# The Fitting Room

> *You* (the narrator who launders his own monstrousness) meets a *Gone Girl* orchestration — but the wife never raises her voice and the gun never goes off.

## Logline
A self-deceiving Bath architect spends one closing-time hour buying his wife an anniversary scarf, narrating himself as the devoted husband — never grasping that the shopgirl is his mistress, that the silk is a message, and that his wife found out months ago and has spent them befriending the other woman and authoring the exit he is walking home toward.

## Central dramatic question
What does Clare already know, and what is she orchestrating? — and beneath it: will Daniel ever see himself truly? (He won't. That refusal is the gut-punch.)

## Theme (as argument)
You cannot be confronted into seeing yourself. The man who has rewritten his own story will rewrite even his ruin — so the people he wronged are freed not by his confession but by ceasing to wait for it and walking out of the story he keeps telling about them.

## Principal characters
- **Daniel Reeve (46, conservation architect)** — WANT: the perfect anniversary, every compartment sealed and plumb · NEED: to stop narrating and actually see (refused) · WOUND: raised to keep a flawless front over a collapsing family · LIE: if the surface holds, the truth doesn't count; a thing unspoken didn't happen · ARC: **anti-arc** — renarrates ruin as devotion.
- **Mara Doyle (31, boutique alterations / front-of-house)** — WANT: hand over the scarf and finally leave — affair, shop, town, on the last train · NEED: to author her own exit instead of waiting to be chosen · WOUND: a pattern of being the convenient secret, kept never claimed · LIE: being chosen in the dark is being chosen · ARC: waiting in the dark → choosing herself in daylight, catalysed by befriending the wife she wronged.

## Supporting cast
- **Clare Reeve (44, ceramicist / kintsugi)** — the unseen orchestrator; the opposite of the facade; makes the break visible in gold. Kept off-page on purpose.
- **Yusuf Karim (38, conservation officer)** — keeps flagging the crack Daniel calls cosmetic; the inspection plot that rhymes with the marriage.
- **Priya Ferren (52, boutique owner)** — oblivious, kind; hands Mara the closing keys; the innocent stage manager.
- **Greg (Daniel's brother)** — keeper of the family's old silence; shows where the facade was learned.
- **Tom (Mara's brother, Bristol)** — the daylight life at the other end of the train.

## Structure (movements — because/therefore/but)
1. **Closing Time** — Daniel narrates an ordinary errand; *but* the shopgirl knows too much and a woman was in that afternoon; *therefore* the reader senses the scene is not what he says.
2. **The Royal Crescent** — he lays out marriage and (laundered) affair as devotion; *therefore* we see the architecture of the deception; *but* Mara's past reveals the calm woman befriending her is his wife.
3. **Comparing Notes** (midpoint) — the women compare the same lie; *therefore* Clare chooses to leave rather than confront; *but* that means Daniel is the one person who will never know.
4. **The Afternoon** — Clare buys one scarf, they re-tag its twin for him to "find"; *therefore* the lock-the-door moment is recast from seduction to ceremony.
5. **Anniversary** — he carries the twin home rehearsing his speech; *therefore* he reaches an emptied house and an identical scarf on a chair; *but* he relabels even that: *She must have stepped out to surprise me.*

## Chapter map
*№ · title · JOB · movement · POV(s) · B-plots advanced*

1. **Closing Time** — JOB: hook; install the unreliable narrator, the central image (scarf · fitting room · the lock), and the mystery (the afternoon woman; why Mara knows his coffee, his wife's "changed size," his fitting room). Mov.1. POV: **Daniel** (present, boutique) + **Mara** (past: the day a calm woman first walked in). B-plots: Mara's exit seeded; coffee + alteration motifs seeded.
2. **The Royal Crescent** — JOB: build the architecture of self-deception (perfume "tester," burner "for work," receipts "gifts planned"); reveal to the reader that Clare = the woman befriending Mara. Mov.2. POV: **Daniel** (the marriage + the laundered affair) + **Mara** (how it started; recognizing the wife). B-plots: failing frontage seeded (officer Karim); perfume + burner motifs.
3. **Comparing Notes** — JOB (midpoint, real work): the women's friendship and the comparing of notes; Clare decides not to confront but to leave and let him keep his story; kintsugi paid in. Mov.3. POV: **Mara** (dominant) + **Daniel** (present interludes, choosing wrapping, rehearsing). B-plots: kintsugi commission; Mara's exit deepens.
4. **The Afternoon** — JOB: deliver the orchestration's mechanics — Clare's afternoon visit, the two scarves, the re-tag, the goodbye disguised as commerce; recast the frame's lock as ceremony; land Karim's formal notice. Mov.4. POV: **Mara** (the afternoon goodbye) + **Daniel** (present, escalating to the locked door). B-plots: all three converge.
5. **Anniversary** — JOB: climax + the walk home + the emptied house + the final self-deception; pay off every motif; answer the question (she knew everything, authored her exit, is gone, and he still can't see). Mov.5. POV: **Daniel** (climax, walk, home, last line) + brief **Mara** coda (the train out). B-plots: all resolve.

## Themes & tone
Quiet, controlled, dread under glass. The horror is domestic and the violence is only ever to the truth. Tone: a beautiful surface you don't trust; warmth between the two women that aches because of who they are to each other; a narrator so smooth he's frightening. The book should make a reader want to turn back to page one and watch every sentence mean its second thing. End on delusion, not punishment.
